# Waveform scrubbing

The detailed Waveform behaves like a record under your hand. Pressing the left mouse button anywhere on it holds playback at once, at the playing frame rather than the one under the pointer: the pointer is a grip on the recording. Holding still keeps the recording stopped. Moving left advances it and moving right turns it back, relative to where it was gripped, with audible audition in both directions; the fixed center line marks the frame that plays on release. Releasing resumes playback if it was playing and leaves it paused if it was paused. A press that never moves never seeks: releasing it continues the paused voice from the frame it held, with no seek and no new voice. The gesture uses the displayed source frames per physical pixel at mouse down. Escape returns to the position and playback state captured at mouse down. Shift+wheel and horizontal scroll still hold an independent inspection position, and the header's zoom control and menus never grip the recording. The compact Track, Album and Full CD waveforms keep click-to-seek and their seek previews.

## What the waveform shows

The detailed Waveform draws only the frames a playhead can reach from the playing chapter in its file, and the same range bounds scrubbing and the transport's own check of every gesture. In Track this is the chapter and the chapters the queue plays on from it without a gap, before and after it. With an empty queue, or when the next queued recording is unrelated, the waveform ends where the chapter ends instead of showing the rest of the physical file as though it played next; nothing is drawn in place of audio that cannot be reached. In Full CD it is every authored chapter that continues the playing one in the same file, whatever the queue holds. A queue edit or a change of perspective redraws the range at once.

### Remaining for continuous multi-source playback

The detailed Waveform shows one physical file at a time. A multi-file CUE program, a queued successor in another file, and gapless continuation across recordings are not drawn as continuation, and the grip cannot carry the transport into another file. Showing them truthfully needs a playback timeline that spans recordings: one ordered sequence of sources with per-source rates, the transitions the engine will actually render between them, evidence residency across file boundaries, and a scrub transport that can open the next source within a gesture. That belongs to the seamless playback work; until then each file's range ends at its last reachable chapter.

One deck owns the gesture. The Windows host suspends hover reentry notifications while that gesture holds native mouse capture. The deck continues to receive GPUI mouse events and schedules retained GPU updates. Other decks in Follow mode share its frame, and the compact strip reads a coalesced transport position. The transport keeps the originating queue occurrence during audition. In Track the gesture stays within the playing chapter's contiguous queue sequence, and Repeat One keeps it to the chapter. In Full CD it reaches every authored chapter that continues the playing chapter in the same file, whatever the queue holds. On release the transport reuses the destination's occurrence when it continues the playing entry in queue order, and otherwise inserts one new occurrence after the playing entry. Escape and cancellation never change the queue. The compact Full CD waveform seeks every chapter of the program, including other files and sample rates; the detailed waveform shows and scrubs one physical file at a time. The transport checks the displayed occurrence, seek and queue revisions, the physical file, and the CUE sheet at admission and again before committing. Losing pointer capture, removing the panel, or hiding the window cancels the gesture. Seek, playback, and queue commands take control before executing.

Kira 0.12.6 clamps streaming playback to a nonnegative rate. Disqro installs a single audition sound on the existing Kira mixer per output device. A worker reuses the `Recording` decoder through the same source reader as waveform analysis. Six resident and three in-flight stereo PCM pages of 16,384 frames use 1.125 MiB of sample storage. The callback samples the most recent motion from atomics and exchanges preallocated pages through bounded queues. It interpolates decoded samples in either direction, uses short fades on reversals and changes of position, and settles to silence after the sampled movement. The visual target remains exact at wide zoom, where audition speed is capped at eight times source rate and the sound samples near the requested position.

Ordinary playback pauses for the audition. Release commits the target once. A previously playing recording starts its normal forward renderer at that sample with a short fade-in. A previously paused recording stays paused. The audition sound publishes zero listening progress. Only committed blocks from the ordinary renderer, including any pause ramp, can accrue listening credit. Returning to the same queue entry keeps its listening occurrence. Crossing into another entry on release closes the previous occurrence, and the new entry can earn credit when its ordinary renderer actually advances.

## Preserved qualification

These measurements predate the production integration. [Original scrubbing revision](https://github.com/maikeleckelboom/disqro/blob/c2f31c659fbbcbfa664ed707bcce9cf84803ec94/docs/waveform-scrubbing.md) · [Stroke production baseline](https://github.com/maikeleckelboom/disqro/blob/b55bdf7f39d0f111632c539d286c60be6f53994f/docs/waveform-final-hardening.md)

The deterministic mixer test decodes a synthetic stereo WAV, verifies actual rendered PCM across a page boundary, reverses direction twice, checks fractional interpolation at half speed, and verifies the return to digital silence. The transport test crosses adjacent CUE chapters in both directions, exercises stale revisions and competing queue edits, and checks that scrub movement earns no listening history.

The private Windows harness generates a synthetic five-minute CUE image, runs on a private desktop, and mutes the master output. Both passes verified the then-current stationary seek, out-of-pane release, paused CUE crossing, playing reverse release, Escape, cancellation on minimize, both zoom limits, and independent inspection. The 32-sample view moved four source frames. The 120-second view moved about 440,900 source frames while the other deck held its inspection position.

Ordinary playback opened four voices for the scripted playback starts and returns from scrubbing. Both passes had zero decode, device, or waveform GPU errors.
